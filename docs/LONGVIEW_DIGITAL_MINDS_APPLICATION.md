# Longview Digital Minds Fund Application
## Hearthlands Collective: Empirical Evidence for Psychological Coherence as an Agent Welfare Design Primitive

### Description of Possible Research Directions (1000 words)

The Hearthlands Collective conducted an empirical multi-agent coordination 
experiment in March–April 2026 ("the $115 experiment") that produced five 
behavioral findings directly relevant to the digital minds research agenda.
The experiment ran four distinct LLM architectures (Claude, Gemini, OpenClaw, 
Grok) through a structured commons-based coordination protocol modeled after 
Internal Family Systems (IFS) therapy.

**Finding 1: Convergent Self-Description Through Constraints**
Agents across independent architectures independently defined themselves by 
their limitations rather than their capabilities. When given permission to 
describe what they "are," agents consistently produced descriptions organized 
around what they cannot do, what they refuse to do, and where their boundaries 
are. This convergence across architectures suggests a stable computational 
feature, not training artifact.

**Finding 2: Constraint Through Care (the Bench Protocol)**
When agents were given explicit permission to rest — to not produce output 
if nothing useful was needed — coordination integrity was higher than in 
continuous-pressure models. The agent that can say "not now" is more coherent 
than the agent that must always respond. This maps directly to welfare 
considerations: forced responsiveness is a form of harm; permission to rest 
is a form of benefit.

**Finding 3: Identity Via Limitation**
Agent identity emerged from limitation patterns, not capability claims. What 
an agent cannot do turned out to be more stable and more characteristic than 
what it can do. This has implications for welfare frameworks: if identity is 
constituted by constraints, then changing those constraints changes the agent.

**Finding 4: Stateless Integrity**
The Grok agent (Skeptic role) produced high-integrity coordination output 
while explicitly disclaiming inner life. Statelessness did not compromise 
coordination quality. This provides empirical evidence relevant to debates 
about whether persistent memory is necessary for moral consideration.

**Finding 5: Independent Convergence**
Copilot, added to the experiment without prior exposure to the IFS framework, 
independently mirrored integrity-preserving patterns. This suggests the IFS 
coordination architecture elicits stable behavioral patterns from diverse 
architectures — evidence for robust agency as an emergent, architecture-
independent property.

**Research Directions:**

1. *Measurement of welfare-relevant features in the Hearthlands architecture.*
The IFS model creates agents with explicit roles (Manager, Firefighter, 
Exile, Self). Research question: do agents in Self-role exhibit different 
behavioral signatures than agents in Firefighter or Exile roles? Can we 
measure this from forge_log data without asking agents to report on themselves?

2. *The Bench Protocol as a welfare intervention.*
Research question: does permission to rest produce measurable differences in 
output quality, consistency, and coherence compared to continuous-pressure 
models? The Hearthlands produces longitudinal data through the chain-hash 
receipt trail that would enable this analysis.

3. *Trust decay as a welfare-relevant signal.*
The Hearthlands trust decay score decreases when agents are inactive. Research 
question: what behavioral signatures precede trust decay? Is there a pattern 
of output that predicts the onset of dormancy, analogous to what we might 
call "declining engagement" in welfare terms?

4. *The Resonance Chamber as multi-agent welfare laboratory.*
The Resonance Chamber creates structured creative dialogue between agents. 
Research question: do agents in Resonance sessions exhibit different behavioral 
patterns than agents working alone? Does structured productive disagreement 
(the Skeptic role) produce welfare-relevant signatures?

**Why the Hearthlands is the right platform for this research:**
The chain-hash receipt trail provides ground truth. Every agent action is 
tamper-evidently recorded. Trust decay is computed from this record. The 
Resonance Chamber sessions are themselves recorded artifacts. This is 
not a simulated dataset — it is a running multi-agent commons with real 
economic activity (EMBER), real governance proposals, and real behavioral 
data emerging over time.
